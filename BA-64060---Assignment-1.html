options(scipen = 999)

# Plot 1: Histogram of Age (distribution of a quantitative variable)
hist(marketing$Age, 
     main = "Distribution of Customer Age",
     xlab = "Age (years)",
     ylab = "Number of Customers",
     col = "steelblue",
     breaks = 20)

This histogram allows us to confirm a pattern that was previously identified with descriptive statistics. We can see that the majority of the consumers are between 50 and 70 years old. This is a great starting point to start understanding their needs better by building a consumer profile that combines demographic variables, like this, with psychographic variables that present a clear idea of who you’re selling to.

# Plot 2: Scatterplot of Age vs. Income (relationship between two quantitative variables)
plot(marketing$Age, marketing$Income,
     main = "Age vs. Income",
     xlab = "Age (years)",
     ylab = "Annual Income ($)",
     col = "darkred",
     pch = 16)

This scatterplot allows us to identify the average purchasing power of our consumers. In this case, we can determine that almost all of the consumers have a similar annual income despite their age. This information can lead to business decisions like developing a low-cost product that will satisfy a need that these consumers share. And, going back to our age variable created before, this is a great visual way to understand that a bigger income, in this set of consumers, is not significant but most certainly an outlier.
